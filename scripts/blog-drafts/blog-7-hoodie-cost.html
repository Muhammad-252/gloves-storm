What drives the price of a hoodie

  • Fabric weight: a 450–500 gsm French terry uses more cotton than a 350–400 gsm fleece, which is why heavyweight hoodies cost more.
  • Construction: a zip, a boxy drop-shoulder fit or a double-layer hood adds material and sewing time.
  • Quantity: pattern and cutting costs are shared across more pieces, so the price drops at 100 and 300 pieces.
  • Decoration: one print or embroidery is included; each extra position or technique is added per piece.
  • Finishing: washes, custom labels and branded packaging each add a small amount per piece.
  • Plus sizes: sizes 4XL and above carry a 10% surcharge because they use more fabric.

How to lower your cost per hoodie

1

Order 100 pieces, not 90

Crossing into the 100–299 tier saves $5.25 per heavyweight hoodie, which can make a bigger order cheaper overall.

2

Keep the first drop simple

Use the included print or embroidery and add one statement detail, rather than four decoration techniques.

3

Choose the right fabric weight

A 350–400 gsm fleece pullover at $32 suits everyday hoodies; save 450–500 gsm French terry for premium pieces.

4

Combine styles in one order

Order hoodies with matching sweatpants in the same fabric and colors to keep the look consistent.
