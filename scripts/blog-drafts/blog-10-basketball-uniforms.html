Standard or reversible?

A standard uniform is a single layer of mesh with one design. It is the lightest option and the best value for teams that only need one kit, or that order separate home and away sets.

A reversible uniform is made from two layers of mesh, each fully sublimated with its own design, names and numbers. Players turn it inside out for the second color. It costs $10 more per set than a standard uniform, but it replaces two separate uniforms, which is why leagues, camps and training programs that need two looks often choose it.

Fabric, necklines and fit

  • Fabric: breathable polyester mesh or dazzle, 160–220 gsm, chosen for fast sweat drying and four-way movement.
  • Neckline: V-neck, round neck or a ribbed contrast neckband.
  • Seams: double-needle stitching on armholes and hems, where basketball jerseys wear out first.
  • Shorts: an elastic waistband with an inner drawcord, with or without pockets.

Numbers, names and decoration

Full sublimation is included, so your design can use any number of colors, gradients and patterns, and every player gets their own name and number at no extra cost. If you want a classic stitched look, tackle twill numbers and letters are available and quoted per design.

Check your competition's rules before you finalize numbers. FIBA's Official Basketball Rules, for example, allow the numbers 0, 00 and 1 to 99, while school and local leagues may set their own limits.

Sizing

Uniforms are graded from youth extra small to adult 5XL, and sizes can be mixed within your 30 sets. Sizes 4XL and above carry a 10% surcharge. Basketball players often prefer a looser fit, so it is worth checking the sample on a couple of players before bulk production.
