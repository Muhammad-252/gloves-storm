What drives the price of a team uniform

  • The sport and the cut: a soccer jersey is a simple, light shirt. An American football jersey has a pro cut and stretch shoulders, and football pants need 4-way stretch fabric and pad pockets, so they cost more to make.
  • Number of pieces in the uniform: a soccer or basketball set is two pieces; adding socks, a goalkeeper kit or warm-ups adds to the per-player cost.
  • Quantity: pattern and setup costs are shared across more uniforms, so the price drops about 10% at 100 pieces and again at 300.
  • Decoration method: full sublimation is included, so the number of colors in your design does not change the price. Tackle twill or embroidery on football jerseys is quoted per design.
  • Special builds: reversible basketball uniforms use two layers of mesh and two designs, which is why they cost more than a standard set.
  • Large sizes: sizes 4XL and above carry a 10% surcharge because they use more fabric.

How to lower your cost per uniform

1

Order 100, not 90

Because the 100-piece price is 10% lower, 90 soccer kits ($1,890) cost exactly the same as 100 ($1,890). From about 90 pieces, the extra 10 uniforms are effectively free, which is handy for spares and new players.

2

Use one design across teams

The minimum is 30 pieces per design, not per team. Two small teams that share a club design reach the minimum together, and each player still gets their own name and number.

3

Go full sublimation

With sublimation, a design with ten colors costs the same as one with two, and the print never cracks or peels. It is the most cost-effective way to get a bold, custom look.

4

Order everything in one shipment

Add socks, tracksuits or training tops to the same order. One shipment and one customs entry usually costs less than several small ones.
