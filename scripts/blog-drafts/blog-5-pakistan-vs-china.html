Most brands sourcing apparel overseas end up comparing the same two countries. China has the largest garment supply chain in the world. Pakistan is smaller, but it has its own cotton, spinning and knitting base, and Sialkot has made sports goods and teamwear for international brands for generations. This guide compares the two on the things that decide a first order: minimums, fabric, cost, duties, lead times and communication.

Minimum order quantities

MOQ is usually the first wall a new brand hits. Large Chinese mills are built for volume, so many quote several hundred pieces per color for full custom work. Smaller Chinese workshops and trading companies go lower, but you often lose direct contact with the people sewing your product.

Sialkot factories tend to run smaller batches because teamwear orders are naturally small: a club needs 20 to 40 kits, not 2,000. That is why low-MOQ production is normal there. Fabric and product strengths

Match the country to the product, not the other way round.

  • Heavyweight cotton streetwear: Pakistan's cotton base suits 400–500 GSM French terry hoodies, heavyweight tees and sweatpants.
  • Sublimated teamwear: Sialkot is a major source of soccer kits, basketball uniforms and American football uniforms.
  • Gym and activewear: both countries make nylon-spandex gym wear; China has more fabric choice, Pakistan more flexibility on small runs.
  • Technical outerwear: bonded seams, waterproof membranes and specialty insulation are usually easier to source in China.

Cost, duties and landed price

Compare landed cost, not the factory price. Landed cost is the unit price plus freight, import duty, customs fees and any rework. A cheaper unit price can lose its advantage once duties are added.

  • Selling in the USA: many Chinese-made garments carry Section 301 tariffs on top of normal duty. Check the HTS code for your product before you compare quotes.
  • Selling in the EU: Pakistan is a GSP+ beneficiary, which gives many textile and apparel lines reduced or zero EU import duty. Check that your product and its origin rules qualify.
  • Selling in the UK: Pakistan is covered by the UK Developing Countries Trading Scheme. Check the current tariff for your commodity code.

Trade rules change, so confirm current duty rates with your customs broker or freight forwarder. With DDP shipping the factory quotes a delivered price that already includes duty, so there are no surprises at the port.

Lead times and shipping

China has the advantage on ocean freight routes and port capacity. For small orders, though, most brands ship by air, and the difference in transit time is small. What matters more is how fast a factory can sample.

Communication and quality control

English is widely used in Pakistan's export business, which makes it easier to talk directly to the people who make your product instead of going through an agent. Whichever country you choose, ask for the same quality controls: a pre-production sample you approve, inline checks during sewing, and a final inspection against an AQL standard before shipping.

How to choose

1

List your products

Cotton fleece, tees and teamwear point to Pakistan. Complex synthetic outerwear points to China. Many brands use both.

2

Price the landed cost

Get delivered (DDP) quotes from both, with duty included for your market.

3

Order samples from two factories

Judge fabric hand-feel, stitching and fit side by side before committing to bulk.
