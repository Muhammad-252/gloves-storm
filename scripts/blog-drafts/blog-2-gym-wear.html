Understanding Activewear Fabrics: The Decision That Defines Your Brand

In technical activewear, the fabric is the product. If a customer tests your leggings during a deep squat and finds the rear sheer, or if your training top traps sweat odor, they will never repurchase. Before you contact any private label clothing manufacturer, you must master the fundamental textile blends:

1. Nylon-Spandex (Nylon/Lycra) — The Gold Standard Benchmark

Premium Tier

An 80/20 or 75/25 Nylon-Spandex blend is the fabric that industry leaders like Lululemon and Gymshark built global reputations upon. Nylon filament delivers buttery-soft hand feel, incredible abrasion resistance, and superior elastic recovery that hugs the body without sagging at the knees. It retains color through hundreds of high-temperature washes.

Optimal Target: 220–240 GSM for compression leggings; 190–210 GSM for matching sports bras.

2. Polyester-Spandex — The High-Output Performance Mid-Tier

Versatile Tier

An 88/12 or 85/15 Polyester-Spandex blend balances technical moisture-wicking with exceptional cost efficiency (25–35% lower unit cost than nylon). Polyester fibers disperse moisture across micro-capillaries for rapid drying and accept vibrant all-over sublimation printing. It is the premier choice for custom moisture-wicking performance t-shirts and custom athletic shorts.

3. Cotton-Spandex — The Athleisure & Studio Blend

Lifestyle Tier

A 95/5 Combed Cotton-Spandex blend bridges technical gym wear and lifestyle lounge apparel. It offers natural organic breathability and zero static cling. Because cotton retains moisture, it is unsuitable for high-intensity HIIT training, but perfectly tailored for pilates, yoga, and relaxed streetwear loungewear.

4. Recycled Performance Fabrics (REPREVE® & rPET)

Eco-Certified

Post-consumer recycled polyester certified by REPREVE® transforms recycled plastic bottles into technical yarn. For brands targeting the UK and European fitness demographics where eco-responsibility drives purchasing decisions, incorporating certified recycled activewear provides a potent marketing narrative.

What GSM Actually Means — And The "Squat Test" Rule

GSM (grams per square metre) indicates physical textile weight and density. Asking for "gym leggings" without specifying GSM allows factories to quote on cheap 170 GSM sheer cloth. Here is the operational calibration:

  • 160–190 GSM (Lightweight): Engineered for lightweight running tops, gym tank tops, and breathable base layers. Too thin for single-layer compression leggings.
  • 220–240 GSM (Mid-to-Heavy Performance): The gold standard for squat-proof gym leggings, high-support sports bras, and biker shorts. Delivers complete opacity under tensile stretch while supporting muscle recovery.
  • 250–280 GSM (Sculpting Heavyweight): Used for high-compression sculpting tights, seamless thermal base layers, and luxury athleisure where structural hold is paramount.

Seam Engineering: Why Flatlock Stitching Is Non-Negotiable

Budget garment factories cut costs by using standard 3-thread overlock seams. On activewear, raised overlock seams create abrasive ridges inside the thigh and waistband, causing severe chafing during workouts.

Professional activewear requires 4-needle 6-thread flatlock stitching (ISO 607 stitch type). Flatlock machines butt fabric edges together without overlapping, locking them into a completely smooth, flush seam that stretches seamlessly with the fabric.
