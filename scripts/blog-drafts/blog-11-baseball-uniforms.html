Jersey styles

  • Full-button: the classic pro look, buttoned all the way down the front.
  • Two-button: a Henley-style top that pulls on over the head, popular for youth and softball teams.
  • Pullover V-neck: no buttons at all, quick to put on and easy to sublimate edge to edge.

All three can have contrast piping along the placket, collar and sleeves in your team colors, and a curved drop-tail hem that stays tucked in.

Fabric and durability

Baseball jerseys are made from 200–250 gsm polyester warp-knit or double-knit, heavier than a soccer shirt so they stand up to slides and dirt. Pants use a stretch poly/spandex blend with belt loops, and can have reinforced knees, piping or a side stripe to match the jersey. You can choose an open hem for a long pant look or an elastic hem for a knicker style.

Team script and numbers

There are two ways to put your team name on the chest:

  • Sublimation (included): the script, numbers and any pattern, such as camo or pinstripes, are dyed into the fabric. There is no limit on colors and nothing to peel.
  • Tackle twill: cut fabric letters stitched onto the jersey for the traditional stitched look. It is heavier and is quoted per design.

High school teams in the USA play under National Federation rules, which include uniform requirements. Check the current NFHS baseball rules or your league's rules before finalizing colors and numbers.

Sizing

Sizes can be mixed freely within each 30-piece order, from youth to adult. Sizes 4XL and above carry a 10% surcharge. Jerseys and pants are separate items, so you can order different size splits for each, which helps with players who wear a different size top and bottom.
